Silenced Saints PREACH THE FORGOTTEN · SERMON KIT 7

One-page preaching outline

The Book in the Rubble

Huldah · 2 Kings 22:14–20

Primary text: 2 Kings 22:14–20 Also read: 2 Kings 22:1–23:30 · 2 Chronicles 34:1–33 · Deuteronomy 28–29 · Jeremiah 31:31–34 · Galatians 3:10–14 Big idea: The word of God found in the rubble wounds the proud, hears the humble, and points to the King who took its curse: Huldah spoke the word, Josiah reformed the nation, and Jesus bore the judgment. Time: 55–60 minutes, including a 3–5 minute response (6,858 words at about 120 wpm). Aim: From reading without hearing to letting the Word wound and reform us; from trusting our clean-up to trusting the King who took the curse.

Movement map

Min Section Beats and transition
5 Introduction: The book in the rubble 622 BC. Manasseh’s wreckage; Josiah, eight, does right; the temple is repaired; Hilkiah finds the Book of the Law; Shaphan reads it; the king tears his clothes. The book was in the house; the word was not in the heart. → “Read it.”
7 The king who tore his clothes Manasseh’s idols and the temple’s decay. The reform begins before the find; the word meets him in ordinary work. The covenant curses (Deut 28:15). “For me, and for the people, and for all Judah” (22:13). He believes the book enough to be afraid. → “Who can tell us what God is saying?”
8 The woman in the Second Quarter Five officials—high priest, secretary, king’s servant, two more—go to Huldah (22:14). Prophetess; wife of Shallum, keeper of the wardrobe; the Mishneh. “Thus says the LORD… Tell the man who sent you” (22:15). God is not bound to expected channels. The messenger is tested by the message. → “She speaks.”
8 The word she spoke Judgment on “this place,” grounded in the book (22:16–17); the temple is no talisman (Jer 7:4). Then mercy because “your heart was tender” (22:19). Rakh: soft, woundable. “A grave in peace” — Josiah dies at Megiddo (23:29–30) but is buried before the fall. → “Certain judgment; personal mercy.”
7 The reform that could not save Covenant (23:1–3), purge (23:4–20), Passover (23:21–23). “No king like him” (23:25). Yet the wrath is not turned away (23:26–27). The law cannot atone; the heart is the problem (Gal 3:10–13). New covenant, new heart (Jer 31:33; Ezek 36:26–27). → “The best reform cannot save.”
8 The King who took the curse Jesus the true King: reads the book (Luke 4:21), cleanses the temple (John 2:19), becomes the Passover (1 Cor 5:7), is the Prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15; Acts 3:22). He takes the curse (Gal 3:13; 2 Cor 5:21). No condemnation (Rom 8:1); the Spirit writes the law within (Rom 8:3–4). → “Come to the King.”
6 Application: What do you do with a book that finds you? Let the Word wound you before it comforts you. Bring your fear to the place where God speaks. Do not despise the messenger. Do not stop at mourning; do the reform — but trust the King, not the reform. → “Grace, then obedience.”
3 The invitation Three people: the one who reads without hearing; the one hiding from the book; the one who has mourned but never come to the King. → “His name is Jesus. He saves.”
5 Response and closing Silence. Three questions: what have you avoided, whose voice have you dismissed, what reform is asked? One act this week. Care note for victims (Ps 34:18). Prayer. The book found Josiah; the King finds us. → “Go and live under the book.”

Total: 57 minutes; 55–60 with normal variation.

Lines to land

  • “The book was in the house of God; the word was not in the heart of the people.”
  • “Your heart was tender.”
  • “The judgment is certain; the mercy is personal.”
  • “The best reform in Judah’s history could not turn away the wrath.”
  • “The book found Josiah. The King finds us.”

Guardrails

  • Do not invent the discovery scene or Huldah’s biography; the text gives the delegation, names, Second Quarter, and oracle.
  • Say “widely identified with Deuteronomy or a core,” not “we know what the scroll was.”
  • Do not turn “a grave in peace” into prosperity; Josiah died in battle at Megiddo.
  • Note Huldah’s significance without a slogan; label rabbinic traditions as later.
  • The gospel must be explicit: the cross, the resurrection, and the new heart. Do not end on “read your Bible more.”

If you must cut

  • To 50 minutes: trim 7 minutes; keep 22:19–20, the tender heart, Megiddo, 23:26–27, Galatians 3:13, and the empty tomb.
