A complete sermon for the pulpit — not another study.

“The Man Who Pulled Down the House” is a 6,874-word sermon on Judges 16:4–31, with Judges 13–15 in view. The strongest man in Israel is asleep in the wrong lap, telling the one secret that was never his to trade. He wakes to a room full of enemies and a God whose Spirit has gone. He grinds grain in a prison, blind and shorn. Then he prays the smallest prayer in the story — and brings the temple down on himself.

It is written to be preached, not merely read. The manuscript has an introduction, five movements, application, an invitation, and a guided response. It faces the violence and the suicide honestly, without turning a body count into a trophy. The outline gives you timings and transition lines. The slides are editable. The small-group guide lets the sermon keep working after Sunday.

Who it is for

Pastors, church planters, bi-vocational preachers, guest preachers, and teachers who want a faithful, finished draft to make their own. If you have ever mistaken a gift for a source, or reached for strength that had already gone, this sermon is for you.

Handling a hard text

Samson’s death is a tragedy, not a model. The manuscript says so plainly and includes a pastoral care note for anyone in danger of self-harm. It also keeps the gospel at the center: Samson pulled down Dagon’s house with his own hands and died in the rubble; Jesus let his own body be pulled down and rose from it. The application moves from the text to the heart — name the gift, do not confuse the sign with the source, tell the truth about your eyes, and pray the small prayer.
