A complete sermon for the pulpit — not another study.

“The One-Verse Deliverer” is a 6,722-word sermon on Judges 3:31, with Deborah’s song in Judges 5:6–8 in view. The Bible gives Shamgar one sentence: a name, a father, an enemy, a number, a farm tool, and a verdict. No birth, no call, no speech, no tomb. And yet the sentence ends with the words every judge in the book is measured by: “he also saved Israel.”

It is written to be preached, not merely read. The manuscript has an introduction, five movements, application, an invitation, and a guided response. It takes the brevity seriously instead of padding it, faces the debated name and the violence honestly, and keeps the focus on the God who saves through the ordinary. The outline gives you timings and transition lines. The slides are editable. The small-group guide lets the sermon keep working after Sunday.

Pastors, church planters, bi-vocational preachers, guest preachers, and teachers who want a faithful, finished draft to make their own. If you have ever felt invisible, waited for a better tool, or measured your life by mentions, this sermon is for you.

Handling a one-verse text

Shamgar’s story is one sentence, and the sermon does not invent the rest. It reads the verse alongside Deborah’s song, names the debate over “son of Anath,” and treats the six hundred as a summary rather than a stunt. It also keeps the gospel at the center: Shamgar saved a nation with an oxgoad, and Jesus saved the world with a cross. The application moves from the text to the heart — work the field you were given, use the tool in your hand, refuse the comparison of mentions, and trust the God who counts what the record skips. A pastoral care note is included for victims of violence.
