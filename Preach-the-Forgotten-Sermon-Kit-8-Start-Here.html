Sermon Kit 8: Ezekiel — “The Valley Breathes”

The short version

Read the manuscript once. Make it yours in the DOCX. Rehearse it aloud with the outline. Preach it. The slides and guide support the sermon; they are not the sermon.

What’s in the folder

  • 00 · Start Here — this sheet.
  • 01 · Preaching Manuscript — 6,810 words in two formats. PDF to read; DOCX to edit.
  • 02 · One-Page Outline — movements, timings, lines to land, and the 50-minute cut.
  • 03 · Small-Group Guide — eight questions and leader notes for a follow-up group.
  • 04 · Slides (PPTX) — twelve 16:9 slides, editable in PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides.
  • 05 · Slides (PNG) — the same twelve slides as images for a projector or video.
  • 06 · Preaching License — one page. Keep it with your order records.

Make it yours

  • Work in the DOCX, not the PDF. Change names, local details, and illustrations. Put your own stories in your own voice.
  • Keep the evidence. The dry bones, the two-stage animation, the ruach wordplay, and the promise of opened graves are doing theological work, and the cross and the empty tomb are the turn.
  • Update the title page if you change the title or primary text.
  • The deck is fully editable. All text is live. Georgia sets the headings and Candara the body; if either is missing on your machine, swap both for fonts you have.
  • Read it aloud once before Sunday. If a sentence trips you, change it.

Guardrails

  • Do not reduce the vision to a metaphor for discouragement. The bones are Israel in exile, and the language reaches to the final resurrection.
  • Keep the primary referent: the return from Babylon. Do not claim Ezekiel 37 directly predicts the resurrection of Jesus without showing the canonical steps.
  • Do not merge “son of man” in Ezekiel with Daniel 7 or with Jesus’ title without noting the difference.
  • Do not promise every valley will end on your timetable. The text promises God can raise the dead, not that every prayer resolves quickly.
  • The gospel must be explicit: the cross, the empty tomb, and the Spirit. Do not end on “try harder.”
  • Keep the care note for grief, depression, and suicidal thoughts; safety comes before application.
