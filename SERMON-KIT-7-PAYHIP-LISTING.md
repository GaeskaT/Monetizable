## Full description

**A complete sermon for the pulpit — not another study.**

“The Book in the Rubble” is a 6,858-word sermon on 2 Kings 22:14–20. Josiah’s workmen find the Book of the Law in the temple. The king tears his clothes. Five officials go to a woman in the Second Quarter, and Huldah speaks the word of the LORD: judgment on “this place,” mercy because the king’s heart was tender. The sermon follows the find, the delegation, the oracle, the best reform in Judah’s history, and the greater King who took the curse the law pronounced. It takes the text seriously, faces the hard promise of “a grave in peace,” and lands on the cross, the empty tomb, and the new heart.

It is written to be preached, not merely read. The manuscript has an introduction, five movements, application, an invitation, and a guided response. It gives you timings and transition lines, keeps the gospel explicit, and treats the historical questions with care instead of pretending they are settled. The slides are editable. The small-group guide lets the sermon keep working after Sunday.

**What’s included**

- **Preaching manuscript** — 6,858 words, about 57 minutes with the full response (55–60 with normal variation). An 18-page PDF to read and an editable DOCX to make your own.
- **One-page outline** — movements, timings, lines to land, guardrails, and a 50-minute cut.
- **12-slide deck** — editable 16:9 PPTX for PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides.
- **12 slide images** — the same slides as 1280×720 PNGs for a projector or video.
- **Small-group guide** — eight questions and leader notes for a follow-up group.
- **One-page preaching license** — permission to preach, edit, record, and stream in one congregation or campus. It does not expire.
- **Start Here sheet** — what is in the folder, how to edit the manuscript, and how to take it to the pulpit.
- **Instant download** — one ZIP file with everything above.

**The sermon, not the study**

The Forgotten Figures Field Guide is a nine-session study for groups. This is one finished sermon for the pulpit. It stands on its own; you do not need the study to preach it.

**Built for the preacher**

- Edit freely in the DOCX. Change names, local details, and illustrations. Put your own stories in your own voice.
- Keep the evidence: the Book of the Law, the five-man delegation, Huldah’s oracle, “your heart was tender,” Megiddo, and the reform that could not turn away the wrath are doing theological work, and the cross and empty tomb are the turn.
- Preach it once or return to it years later. The license does not expire.
- Record or stream it as part of your congregation’s normal services.

**Who it is for**

Pastors, church planters, bi-vocational preachers, guest preachers, and teachers who want a faithful, finished draft to make their own. If you have ever read the Bible without hearing it, hidden from a word that found you, or cleaned up the outside while the heart stayed untouched, this sermon is for you.

**Handling a hard text**

The search for the Book of the Law, the identity of the scroll, Huldah’s place in the narrative, the promise that Josiah would be gathered “in peace” before he died at Megiddo, and the tension between Judah’s best reform and the wrath that remained—the sermon names the debated points and says what the text does and does not claim. It also keeps the gospel explicit: the law could not atone; Jesus took the curse. The application moves from the text to the heart—let the Word wound, bring your fear to God, do not despise the messenger, do the reform He asks—without ending on moralism. A pastoral care note is included for victims.
