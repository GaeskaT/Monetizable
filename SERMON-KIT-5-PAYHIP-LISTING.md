## Full description

**A complete sermon for the pulpit — not another study.**

“The Woman Who Ended the War” is a 6,955-word sermon on Judges 4:1–24, with Deborah’s song in Judges 5 in view. Israel has hidden in the hills for twenty years while nine hundred iron chariots own the valley. When God finally routes Sisera’s army at the Kishon, the general abandons his chariot and runs on foot to the one tent he thinks is safe. A woman meets him at the door with a rug, a bowl of milk, and a tent peg.

It is written to be preached, not merely read. The manuscript has an introduction, five movements, application, an invitation, and a guided response. It faces the deception, the broken treaty, and the killing honestly, without turning a household tool into a slogan. The outline gives you timings and transition lines. The slides are editable. The small-group guide lets the sermon keep working after Sunday.

**What’s included**

- **Preaching manuscript** — 6,955 words, about 57 minutes with the full response (55–60 with normal variation). A 17-page PDF to read and an editable DOCX to make your own.
- **One-page outline** — movements, timings, lines to land, and a 50-minute cut.
- **12-slide deck** — editable 16:9 PPTX for PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides.
- **12 slide images** — the same slides as PNGs for a projector or video.
- **Small-group guide** — eight questions and leader notes for a follow-up group.
- **One-page preaching license** — permission to preach, edit, record, and stream in one congregation or campus. It does not expire.
- **Start Here sheet** — what is in the folder, how to edit the manuscript, and how to take it to the pulpit.
- **Instant download** — one ZIP file with everything above.

**The sermon, not the study**

The Forgotten Figures Field Guide is a nine-session study for groups. This is one finished sermon for the pulpit. It stands on its own; you do not need the study to preach it.

**Built for the preacher**

- Edit freely in the DOCX. Change names, local details, and illustrations. Put your own stories in your own voice.
- Keep the evidence: the iron chariots, the Kishon rout, the Kenite treaty, and the tent peg are doing theological work, and the cross and empty tomb are the turn.
- Preach it once or return to it years later. The license does not expire.
- Record or stream it as part of your congregation’s normal services.

**Who it is for**

Pastors, church planters, bi-vocational preachers, guest preachers, and teachers who want a faithful, finished draft to make their own. If you have ever waited for a perfect moment, mistaken peace for passivity, or wondered what is in your hand, this sermon is for you.

**Handling a hard text**

Jael deceives a guest and kills a sleeping man. The manuscript names the horror and the broken treaty plainly; it does not glamorize the killing or turn Jael into a template for private revenge. It also keeps the gospel at the center: Jael drove a peg into the enemy, and Jesus took the nails for his enemies. The application moves from the text to the heart — do not wait for the perfect moment, do not mistake peace for passivity, use what is in your hand, and give the victory to God. A pastoral care note is included for victims of violence.
